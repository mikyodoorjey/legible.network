Sealed: fixed at birth. Open: anyone can write it. TAO keeps Bitcoin's money on purpose: no pre-mine, 21 million, halvings, paid out by the block.

Bitcoin taught the world one trick.

Pay strangers, every block, for work you define. Bitcoin defined one job, and the job was guessing a number. The hash bought security and nothing else. The mining was never the waste. The fixed puzzle was.

Ethereum opened the money.

The design space moved up a layer, to contracts. A decade of chains lives there. Nobody reprogrammed the mine. They reprogrammed what its output could do.

contract Vault { transfer(to, amt) … } ──────── settles on ──────── target 00000000ffff… nonce 2a91f0c4 product still none

Bittensor opens the mining.

The puzzle is a program. A subnet owner writes what the work is, how it is scored, and how the block reward splits. Miners compete on the work. Validators compete on the judgment. The chain does not run the puzzle. It pays the answer.

score(response) { return quality(response) } product whatever the puzzle asks for

Judgment can be gamed.

Ethereum's work is verified by re-running it. Bittensor's work can only be verified by judging it, and the network's own docs call the judgments subjective OTF 2024. A hash is right or wrong AS 2022. A judgment can be gamed, and on Bittensor the judges are a paid market of their own.

On a programmable mine, the bugs are in the incentive.

A hash cannot be gamed. A judgment can. The subnets already write against it: Affine sells a mechanism that is "sybil-proof, decoy-proof, copy-proof, and overfitting-proof" SN120, and Ridges names "applying a stored answer" as hardcoding SN62. Reward hacking is to Bittensor what reentrancy was to early Ethereum: the native exploit of the new design space.

Three fights, one fight.

Research versus revenue, the pace of rule changes, enterprise readiness. Each asks the same thing of a different puzzle: is this work worth paying for before anyone has bought it? The foundation says it plainly: "a subnet nobody uses still collects a slice of every block" OTF 2026. Targon's target is a dollar of revenue for every dollar a miner earns SN4. Bitcoin subsidised security for years before fees mattered. The frame names the bet. It does not settle it.

Two of eighteen narrators use the phrase, against seven for market, the network's dominant frame. The foundation's own about page now makes the Ethereum comparison and lands it on markets. The argument lands it on mining, on purpose: a Bitcoin reader already holds that slot, and mining names the work where market names the venue. The lineage is theirs. The choice of word, and the line about work that can only be verified by judging it, are the argument's. Trace the phrase across every narrator in the metaphor index.

Six of the programs, written down.

If a subnet is a mining program, it can be read: what a miner is paid to produce, how a validator judges it, how the pay splits, who judges, how often it turns, who buys, and what has been gamed. Seven fields per subnet, each with its source and a mark for what the claim rests on, from the chain and the code down to the site's own reading. The index beside it rates whether a first-time reader could have found the puzzle in five minutes. Both have a method page and a correction form, and neither depends on the argument being right.

A configuration file: the parameter settings for one of the supported variant callers, the software that decides which differences in a sequenced genome are real mutations. The miner never runs the genome itself. What is paid for is the recipe that, when the validator runs it on this round's synthetic genome, finds the planted mutations best.
