# Explainers, slice d

Written 2026-09-10 by agent d. Eight entries in data/explainers/: 118, 120, 19, 44, 5, 68, 75, 8. All between 592 and 594 words except 5 and 75, which were already in range, and all pass scripts/check_explainers.py with --links (every Source and Go deeper URL returned 200).

## Metaphor and the hardest thing to explain

- SN118 Ditto: a hiring contest for an AI assistant's filing clerk. Hardest: that the memory harness the subnet rewards is a different thing from the Ditto product with paying users, that nobody buys a harness, and that the site's scoring page and the repository's miner guide give two different locked models, payout splits and margins.
- SN120 Affine: a boxing title fight for AI coding models. Hardest: the min(R, G) score, where a frozen teacher model judges both how useful a challenger's written reasoning is and how much it resembles the teacher's own, and why a dethroned champion keeps a share of the purse.
- SN19 blockmachine: a call centre staffed by freelance operators who set their own price per call. Hardest: what an RPC endpoint is and why a wallet needs one, and that miners are paid only for answers a validator has re-run and matched, with the unclaimed share destroyed.
- SN44 Score: a press-box statistician, except the statisticians are competing computer-vision models. Hardest: the per-Element winner-takes-all payout with weighted sub-scores, a threshold gate and a difficulty factor, and saying honestly that no dated artifact exists on the site.
- SN5 Hone: a programming exam with sealed answers. Hardest: why a validator commits the signed answers to the problem server before it receives the hidden tests, and what anyone does with the graded record, since the materials never say who reads it or what RLVR stands for.
- SN68 NOVA: a bounty board for drug candidates. Hardest: that the winner is picked by two prediction models (Boltz-2 and a modified BoltzGen) rather than by any lab result, and that the prize is emission paid whether or not a drug company asked for that target.
- SN75 Hippius: a self-storage company whose units sit in strangers' garages. Hardest: erasure coding, where a file becomes 30 pieces of which any 10 rebuild it, and the 70/30 bandwidth-to-storage weight formula that lives in repository root files the README never links.
- SN8 Vanta: a stock-picking contest with a live leaderboard. Hardest: separating the subnet, which ranks play-money signals and pays emission against last week's profit, from Taoshi's retail Vanta Trading product with its evaluation fees, and explaining Theta collateral that is slashed and burnt on elimination.

## Where the sources were thinnest

Hone is the thinnest of the eight: a validator README, a design document and a demo miner, with no product page, no price, no customer, no contact address, an unexpanded acronym for its own name, and a dashboard that reads "Waiting for aggregate data" to anything but a browser, so the commodity section could only point at "exported dataset labels" as an inferred output and the Bittensor section had to say nothing is stated. Affine is close behind on documentation: the GitHub repository has no README and the only guide is llms.txt, written for machines, though the live snapshot API gave the one dated, checkable artifact in the slice. Score has no dated artifact at all; its README links a miner guide that returns 404, the working guide and scoring note sit in unlinked folders, and the site's Documentation link goes to an older repository than the one the chain names. Ditto's problem is the opposite, too many sources that disagree: two rulebooks with different models, splits and margins, a scoring page that still says mining is going live, and a chain contact and description that appear nowhere on the site. For Vanta, NOVA and Hippius the site copy was full but the numbers were not: every Vanta dashboard reads "Loading dashboard data" without a browser and no dated funding figure appears in text; NOVA's dashboard renders empty, its partnerships page gives no price, and the chain snapshot showed two active miners; Hippius has a public price list and a dated status page but no customer name, no revenue and an explorer that renders empty. Blockmachine was the best sourced, with a public price list, three named testimonials and a dated buyback ledger linked to Taostats transactions, and its gaps are structural: empty live dashboards, no status page, no service-level agreement, and a miner README that registers on a testnet. Across the slice, the registration cost was stated by none of the eight subnets and had to be read from the chain snapshot, and five of the eight (Hone, NOVA, Vanta, Score, Hippius) had a chain contact or discord field that was empty or matched nothing on the site.
