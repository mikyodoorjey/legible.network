Written 2026-09-10 from data/index.json (verified 2026-09-09) and each subnet's own materials re-fetched the same night. Eight files at data/explainers/{114,34,38,51,53,61,91,97}.md, all 580 to 599 words by the checker's count, all passing scripts/check_explainers.py with --links. The final check re-fetched all 58 Source and Go deeper URLs with the on-disk cache bypassed; every one returned 200.

## One line per subnet

- SN114 SOMA: a packing contest for AI agents. Hardest to explain: that miners submit compression code and run nothing, and that the judges score the trip rather than the suitcase, so tokens saved only count if the agent still finishes the job; the weights (output tokens three times input, ratio credit capped at fourfold) had to be read from the formula because no prose states them.
- SN34 BitMind: a counterfeit-spotting desk that pays its own forgers. Hardest to explain: two kinds of miner in one contest, with the forgers' pay multiplied by how often they fool the detectors, and a nested payout (40, 40, 4, 16 across lanes, then 85, 10, 5 within each) guarded by a 0.01 margin and a 48-hour defending window.
- SN38 ChronoLLM: a shelf of encyclopedias, one frozen at each year. Hardest to explain: lookahead bias to a reader who has never run a backtest, and why a model's cutoff has to be tested with leak probes rather than read off the spine, followed by round-robin duels judged by a large model inside a sealed processor, all in five sentences.
- SN51 Lium: a holiday-let site whose properties belong to strangers. Hardest to explain: that up to 87 percent of emission pays machines nobody is renting, that a single validator run by the team does all the judging, and that no page says what share of a renter's dollar reaches the host.
- SN53 engy: a petrol station whose pump proves the grade. Hardest to explain: the receipt itself, a published fingerprint of the weights plus a compact summary of the model's internal activity that a checker can compare, without a locked chip to lean on; then four weekly pass-or-earn-nothing gates.
- SN61 RedTeam: a lockpicking league. Hardest to explain: that the same subnet pays for both picks and locks (bots that evade detection and code that catches them), that nobody yet pays for the winning entries, and a decay schedule the docs root gives as fourteen days and the dashboard page as fifteen.
- SN91 cascade: a school where the contest is over who writes the textbook. Hardest to explain: that the entry is a data generator, not a model, that the one student is fixed and trained by the owner alone, and a dethroning rule based on a statistical lower bound clearing a margin that shrinks from 2 to 0.5 percent, with two of the subnet's own documents disagreeing on the payout split.
- SN97 Albedo: a boxing title belt (this metaphor is being rewritten by another agent; the line records the one in the file today). Hardest to explain: "trajectory distillation", which the materials use and never define, and a champion crowned by a judge model's yes-or-no checklist at a 2.5 point margin twice, while the dashboard that should prove it renders only "loading".

## Where the sources were thinnest

cascade (SN91) is the thinnest. Its site is empty, the chain URL lands on a testnet page that shows only a title, and there is no product, price, customer type, or support path, so every fact came from the repository: the README, llms.txt, and four docs, and those disagree with each other on whether the reward split halves per rank or is equal; the demo section is an honest negative, since the signed round receipts can only be replayed from the command line. Albedo (SN97) is nearly as thin: the repository has no README, the guides sit in a docs folder nothing links to, the dashboard shows "loading" for every panel to a fetcher, the chat link explains nothing, and the win margin is 2.5 in the guides and 3 in llms.txt. RedTeam (SN61) has a site whose copyright reads 2024 with no figures, a dashboard that renders only its name, and formulas that exist only in a 2024 whitepaper PDF; detection-rate numbers appear only in third-party press, so "How you would know it works" says no artifact exists. ChronoLLM (SN38) rests on four sources; the leaderboard could not be read by a fetcher, institutional pricing has no number, and the claim that the subnet is in a testing phase comes from third parties, not its own pages, so the arXiv paper stands in as the nearest artifact. BitMind (SN34) is well documented on the product side (pricing, docs, a full Incentive.md) but blank on chain, with no URL, description, or Discord, a broken API Reference link with the real reference behind an account, and usage counters that render as zero. SOMA (SN114) documents miners and validators only: no API reference, an undated savings chart, and a May miner guide that predates the July incentive change, so the scoring weights were read from the formula. Lium (SN51) and engy (SN53) were the best sourced, nine and seven live pages each including llms.txt and structured docs; their gaps are on the demand side, since neither publishes a usage or revenue figure, Lium never states the provider's share of rent, and engy's cheat-detection numbers sit in an undated one-pager and the model cannot be tried without a key. Four artifacts across the slice (albedo.tech, dashboard.theredteam.io, leaderboard.chronollm.com, testnet.cascadesub.net) are apps that give a fetcher nothing but a title, and each entry says so rather than describing what a browser might show. No section was written from memory; where a subnet said nothing, the entry says the materials do not say.
