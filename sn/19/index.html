The work

Answered JSON-RPC requests. The miner runs its own full or archive node for one of eleven chains behind a gateway the network supplies, passes a per-chain eligibility battery, sets a price in US dollars per compute unit, and serves whatever customer traffic the gateway routes to it. What is paid for is compute units served at the bid price, checked for correctness afterwards.

own docs · raw.githubusercontent.com

You earn emissions proportional to the CUs you served at your bid price

How it is scored

Work times price. Each epoch the validator reads the gateway's manifest of compute units each miner served, multiplies non-archive and archive units by that miner's own bid prices in dollars, and that dollar figure is the score. Quality is not in the formula; it acts earlier, in the routing score and burst tests that decide how much traffic a node gets. Correctness is checked afterwards against reference nodes.

code · raw.githubusercontent.com · common/scoring/weights.py

consumed_usd = miner.cu_non_archive * miner.price_non_archive + miner.cu_archive * miner.price_archive

How the pay splits

Proportional to dollars consumed. Each miner's weight is its consumed USD over the total. The miner pool is 41 percent of epoch emission. The scale between pool and consumption is unclamped both ways, so the pool is filled exactly and nothing burns, unless the scale exceeds 10: then consumption is paid at face value and the rest goes to burn sink UID 103. Banned coldkeys get zero.

code · raw.githubusercontent.com · common/scoring/weights.py

MINER_EMISSION_PCT = 0.41; scale = miner_pool_usd / total_consumed_usd; they reduce to consumed_i / consumed_total

Sources disagree The whitepaper fetched 2026-09-19 says an emission burn sink removes excess unearned alpha from circulation; the code comment at the pinned commit says the pool-over-consumption cap was removed so that burn is zero every epoch, with burn only on the sanity bound or an epoch with no gateway activity.

Who judges

Nine validators were active on the 2026-09-09 chain snapshot. Commit-reveal is off. A validator never sees customer traffic: it reads the gateway's logs from public storage, re-executes the logged requests against its own reference nodes, sampling 100 percent up to 100,000 per miner by default, and bans a coldkey permanently on one mismatch. It sets weights from the gateway's compute-unit manifest and a price snapshot, once per epoch.

How often it turns

Emission settles every 7,200 blocks, a full day, twenty times the 360 most subnets use. The validator commits once per chain epoch, from the finalized epoch manifest, inside a commit window it computes from the chain's own epoch clock. A new registration is immune from deregistration for 3,600 blocks, about 12 hours.

chain · hyperparams-2026-09-19.json

tempo 7200, immunity_period 3600; validator README: each epoch (7,200 blocks, ~24 hours)

Sources disagree The miner README at commit ef5e876 says validators submit weights on-chain each epoch (~72 minutes); the chain hyperparameters on 2026-09-19 and the validator README at the pinned commit give 7,200 blocks, about 24 hours.

Who buys

Apps that call blockchains, paying per request unit: as low as $1 per million requests at scale, with a calculator putting 100 million requests a month at $165 against Alchemy at $979, and failed requests not billed. The buybacks page lists five settled cycles from May to September 2026, 6,613 alpha bought for $16,296, each row linked to a Taostats proof, the last on 2026-09-17.

What has been gamed

Serving wrong data: a logged response that mismatches the reference node on a deterministic query is a permanent coldkey ban, applied before weights are built. Claiming archive without archive state, or masking the client software: eligibility failures, re-checked continuously. Dodging the burst test: refused or slow requests each cost 20 seconds in the capacity score. Inflated consumption: a pool-to-consumption scale above 10 fails closed.

What it shows

Explained from zero by the index, from the subnet's own materials, for someone who has never heard of Bittensor.

Explained from zero · other · written 2026-09-10

SN19 blockmachine answers questions that apps ask blockchains, like a call centre staffed by freelance operators who set their own price per call and are paid only for answers checked as correct.

The commodity, explained from zero

Every wallet, explorer and trading bot asks a blockchain questions: what is the latest block, what is this balance, did this transaction go through. Those questions go to an RPC endpoint, a server running a full copy of the chain, and running one is expensive. Blockmachine sells access to such servers for eleven chains, Bittensor and Ethereum among them, at a single address per chain. Usage is metered in Request Units, where a simple query costs 1 and a heavy one more.

The price list is public: a keyless free tier at 300 requests a minute, a $9 Standard plan for 5 million units a month, up to $999 for a billion. "Failed requests don't cost you anything." A calculator on the pricing page puts 100 million requests at $165 against $979 at Alchemy, a named competitor. The front page carries testimonials from three customers, including Taostats: "At 15 billion requests a month, we can't afford for our RPC layer to be the weak point."

Why it is on Bittensor at all

The technology page states the case as cost and trust: "Most supply is still centralized, opaque, and economically misaligned." Competing suppliers bid the price down, and every response can be checked. Here the call-centre comparison breaks: a call centre pays operators by the hour, while blockmachine pays only for answers a validator has re-run and matched, and an operator's unclaimed share is destroyed rather than shared.

How the work gets done

Miners, the operators, run their own chain node behind a gateway the network supplies, register it, and set a price in US dollars per unit of work. Customer requests are routed by a score that combines that price with quality, meaning latency, uptime and correctness, and a burst test measures each node under heavy load so strong hardware earns a bigger share. Validators, the auditors, read the gateway logs, re-run samples against reference infrastructure and compare results; a mismatch has no appeal, and the whitepaper provides for permanent bans. Each epoch of about 72 minutes, emission, the subnet's share of newly minted TAO, is paid in proportion to the units each miner served at its bid price.

How you would know it works

The buybacks page: "For every alpha token paid to miners, blockmachine purchases an equal amount of SN19 alpha" for a reserve, and it lists four settled cycles from May to August 2026, 4,812 alpha for $12,328 in total, each row linked to a Taostats transaction, last synced at block 9,033,809 on 2026-09-10. Anyone can call the free endpoint at rpc.blockmachine.io.

What is missing

The live performance and miner dashboards render empty without a browser. There is no status page and no service-level agreement; support is an email and a channel on the shared Bittensor Discord, and the chain's discord field is empty. The miner README registers on a testnet and gives no mainnet cost (about 1 TAO on chain on 2026-09-09). The chain's repository field is a one-file page linking to the real miner and validator repositories, and the chain description's wording appears nowhere on the site.
