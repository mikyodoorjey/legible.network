The work

Attested GPU cards. A miner installs TargonOS, a signed operating system that wipes the machine and attests it to the network on every boot, and keeps confidential virtual machines available for rent. What is paid for is a live, verified card inside an auction class, whether or not a customer is using it. There is no request or response the miner answers.

code · raw.githubusercontent.com · docs/miner/miner.md

Miner emissions are paid per card based on each auction's target price and the amount of compute live on the network

How it is scored

No quality score. Each auction, fetched from the Tower service, names target cards, a target price and a cap in cents per card hour. The pool is target price times target cards; each verified card earns the lesser of pool divided by live cards and the cap, times 1.2, as a fraction of the dollar value of the miner emission pool, itself computed from chain emission and the TAO price.

code · raw.githubusercontent.com · internal/validator/callbacks/weights.go

perMiner := min(pool/nodes, aucInfo.MaxPrice); minerIncentive := (float64(perMiner) * 1.2 * float64(bid.Count)) / (*c.EmissionPool * 100)

How the pay splits

Proportional to verified cards in each auction, capped per card. Weight no auction covers is burned: split among the burn keys Tower names, remainder to uid 28. If payouts exceed the pool, every weight scales down pro rata. A node reporting fewer cards than the auction's min_cluster_size, 8 for the H200 and B200 classes in the docs example, earns nothing from that auction.

forBurn := max(setup.U16MAX-sumScores, 0); finalUids = append(finalUids, uint16(28))

Who judges

Seven validators were active on the 2026-09-09 chain snapshot, against five miners. Commit-reveal is on, weights at most once per 100 blocks. A validator re-attests each miner node during the interval, counts its GPU cards against the auction's cluster minimum, prices it from Tower's auction table, and posts the weight extrinsic through a weights-api sidecar. Nothing about the compute's quality is judged, only its presence and attestation.

How often it turns

Emission settles every 360 blocks, about 72 minutes, and the validator works in the same interval: it fetches auctions and the emission pool at the start, polls miner nodes every 30 blocks, and sets weights once per interval. A new registration is immune from deregistration for 7,520 blocks, about 25 hours, half again the usual 5,000.

tempo 360, immunity_period 7520

Who buys

Renters of confidential GPU and CPU machines by the hour, through the site and an unauthenticated inventory API. Prices run from $0.45 an hour for one RTX 4090 to $60 for an eight-card B300 class. On 2026-09-19 the inventory listed 33 SKUs, of which 7 showed any availability. No customer is named; the front page shows a logo strip and counters that read zero.

What has been gamed

Free compute drew abuse. The May 2025 post says subsidised inference on other companies' models attracts a queue that leaves when prices rise, so emission will not fund it. The guard in code is attestation: only nodes that pass TargonOS attestation and report at least the cluster minimum enter an auction, and weight no auction covers burns rather than paying idle registrations.

What it shows

Explained from zero by the index, from the subnet's own materials, for someone who has never heard of Bittensor.

Explained from zero · compute · written 2026-09-10

SN4 Targon rents out computing machines by the hour, like a hotel whose rooms belong to strangers, with each room sealed so that not even the owner can see what the guest is doing inside.

The commodity, explained from zero

The room is a GPU, the graphics processor that does most AI computing. Targon lists three products: rentals (a GPU server a customer logs into), virtual machines, and Targon Virtual Machines, its own product. The last is the point of the subnet. A TVM runs inside a trusted execution environment, a locked box in the processor that encrypts memory and can prove to an outsider what ran inside, so the machine's owner cannot read the guest's data. Intel and Targon co-wrote a whitepaper on this, dated March 23, 2026.

Prices exist. The inventory API, which needs no login, quotes an H200 at $2.49 an hour in its example, and the live API returned $0.45 an hour for an RTX 4090 on September 9. The customer named on the site is a team doing "AI Training and Deployments". Whether anyone rents today is harder to see: the front page's counters for GPUs, uptime and utilization read zero to a fetcher, and every listed machine showed "Out of Stock" on the day of checking. A logo strip names OSS Capital, DCG, Google, Shopify and Bittensor without saying why.

Why it is on Bittensor at all

The mission page's claim is price: "a non-interruptible cloud experience at interruptible prices". Because supply can come from anyone with the right hardware, the whitepaper argues prices become "transparent, market-driven". The sealed room is what makes renting from strangers safe. The comparison breaks here: a hotel guest trusts the hotel, while a Targon guest trusts Intel's chip and a cryptographic proof, and the hotel itself never sees the guest's luggage. No number on the site compares Targon's price to a large cloud's.

How the work gets done

Miners (the room owners) install TargonOS, a signed operating system that wipes the machine, attests to the network on every boot, and re-attests roughly every 72 minutes. The subnet runs auctions: each names a target number of cards, a target price and a ceiling in US dollars per card per hour, and "while an auction is below its card target, every live card earns up to the payment cap". Validators (the judges) hold stake and set the weights that turn those rates into emission, the subnet's share of newly minted TAO; the docs describe the miner side in far more detail than the validator side. Emission not claimed by any auction is burned.

How you would know it works

The public inventory endpoint at api.targon.com/tha/v3/inventory returns live machines, specifications and hourly prices with no login. It is a price list, not a usage figure; the materials do not publish one.

What is missing

The chain contact devs@manifold.inc appears nowhere on the site or docs, which offer only a contact form and Discord. The Discord field on chain is empty. There is no status page (status.targon.com does not resolve), no SLA, and no stated response time. The miner docs give no scoring formula and no deregistration policy, and the registration cost is not stated (0.0005 TAO on chain today). The chain shows 5 active miners and 7 validators.
