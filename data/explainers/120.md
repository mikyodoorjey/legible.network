## In one sentence

SN120 Affine runs a boxing title for AI coding models, where one champion checkpoint holds the belt, each challenger gets a single shot, and the purse is split among the last five champions.

## The commodity, explained from zero

The product is a checkpoint: the saved file of a trained model's weights, the file you download to run the model yourself. Affine's checkpoints are trained for one skill, the work an AI coding agent does when it reads a codebase and picks the next command or edit. Only the champion is published; a loser's weights stay sealed in a private bucket and expire after 60 days.

Who pays for the champion is not stated. The front page offers a chat box, "Talk directly to the reigning SN120 king", and every crowned checkpoint is free to download at models.affine.io with a signed manifest. The materials name no customer, price, or use beyond the chat; the money here is the subnet's emission, its share of newly minted TAO, not revenue.

Here the boxing comparison breaks: a dethroned boxer gets nothing, but a dethroned Affine champion keeps a share of the purse while it remains among the last five kings.

## Why it is on Bittensor at all

The materials do not compare Affine with a company's own training run. What they claim is who may enter and who may check. Anyone with a TAO-funded wallet can challenge, and "Replayability is the trust model": two checkpoints, the public corpus and the published scoring code let anyone recompute a verdict. The referee is one validator with its own bucket and eval machine; the chain lists three validators, and the materials do not say how they divide the work.

## How the work gets done

Miners, the fighters, upload a checkpoint to a private bucket and post two signed notices on chain. The validator, the referee, burns the miner's one evaluation slot (one submission per hotkey, the miner's on-chain identity key, ever) and runs a duel: champion and challenger each answer 1,300 coding turns from a public corpus, and a frozen teacher model (Qwen3.8-27B) scores both on min(R, G): how much the model's written reasoning helps the teacher predict the right action, and whether that reasoning looks like the teacher's own. The challenger takes the belt only if its margin clears a floor of two standard errors or 0.002. Emission is split equally among the last five champions, registered hotkeys only, and a published LLM audit reviews every crowning and can revert it.

## How you would know it works

The live snapshot at affine.io/api/v1/snapshot. On 2026-09-10 it showed reign 10, crowned at 02:02 UTC, four challengers queued, and 379 lifetime submissions, 10 accepted and 350 rejected. The front-page chat talks to the reigning model. There is no dated benchmark; the advisory benches the site publishes "never affect Reason or crowning".
